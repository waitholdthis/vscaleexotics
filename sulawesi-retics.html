The locality

Island identity before novelty

Sulawesi is an Indonesian island within the native range of the reticulated python, Malayopython reticulatus. In captive collections, “Sulawesi” should describe documented geographic ancestry—not a color, a morph, or a convenient label for an animal that happens to look different.

Our purpose is preservation-minded: maintain a traceable pair, record their development and temperament, and make no claim that the paperwork cannot support. Locality integrity depends on records across generations.

Founder details are being documented. Names, hatch dates, weights, acquisition history and lineage will appear here only when confirmed. The artwork below is a procedural pattern study, not a photograph of either animal.

The long view

From pair to documented line

We will not turn a hoped-for clutch into a promise. Each stage appears only when it actually happens.

Establish the pair

Confirm provenance, complete veterinary intake, maintain strict quarantine, and establish independent feeding, shed, weight and behavior records.

Earn the pairing

Wait for physical maturity and stable condition. Compatibility, welfare and veterinary judgment take precedence over a calendar or customer demand.

Raise the offspring

If offspring arrive, each baby receives an individual identity, hatch and feeding record, sex where confirmed, locality documentation and a permanent place in the project archive.

What future babies receive

Records before release

  • Individual ID, hatch date, sex and weight series
  • Complete sire and dam record with locality documentation
  • Feeding, shed and health history
  • Established frozen-thawed feeding before placement
  • Species-specific husbandry plan and lifetime support
